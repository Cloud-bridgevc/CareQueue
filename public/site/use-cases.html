Agency use cases

Built around the way home-care offices actually run

Whether you run one branch or twelve, the coordination problems are the same: call-outs, gaps, unconfirmed visits and notes that don't make it to the next shift.

Same-day call-outs

A caregiver calls out at 6 AM with three visits on the schedule.

  • Visits become uncovered and enter the queue ranked by start time.
  • Coverage suggestions rank available caregivers by zone, credentials, prior visits and weekly hours.
  • Reassignment re-checks for overlaps and travel time before you confirm.

EVV and confirmation gaps

Missing check-outs hold up payroll and billing.

  • Late check-ins are flagged while the visit is still running.
  • Unconfirmed visits are escalated after your grace period.
  • Manual confirmations require a reason and are audit-logged.

Shift handovers

The evening coordinator needs to know what the day team left open.

  • Notes are tagged by category and addressed to roles.
  • An AI draft groups open actions and awareness items, citing each note.
  • A supervisor reviews and approves before it is shared.

Multi-branch supervision

Supervisors need one view of what is slipping.

  • Follow-up targets by severity, with overdue items surfaced first.
  • Assignment, acknowledgement and resolution notes on every item.
  • Weekly report of recurring problems by caregiver, client and zone.

Weekly hours and overtime

Part-time caregivers quietly drift past their limit.

  • Scheduled hours are checked against each caregiver's configured limit.
  • Coverage suggestions down-rank caregivers who would exceed it.

Operational incidents

No-shows, access problems and property damage need a paper trail.

  • Report → triage → escalate → resolve with required notes.
  • Follow-up timers feed the supervisor queue when they lapse.
  • Emergencies always go to 911 and your on-call clinical lead first.
